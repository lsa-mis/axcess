## The main table

One table, every detector, every corpus. Source the numbers from the existing
artifacts — **do not re-run anything and do not recompute a single cell**:

- `KAFE-MATRIX.md` and `derived/kafe_matrix_summary.json` — KAFE corpus
- `MATRIX-RESULTS.md` and `derived/matrix.json` — fixtures, gds, ma11y, edgecases

Required columns: detector, corpus, TP, FP, FN, unknown pos, unknown neg,
precision, strict recall, F1, **ms per element/button**, and the timing scope
(what the ms figure actually covers). If the existing artifacts carry a column
the CEO asked for under a different name, map it and say so in a legend.

Rules for the table:

- **No empty cells.** Every cell is a value or a stated reason ("abstained",
  "n/a — corpus has no negatives", etc.). A blank is a defect.
- **C1–C16 appear in the main table**, in detector order, not segregated.
- Where a detector abstained rather than scoring, the reason goes in the cell.
- Keep KAFE's own row clearly marked as the reference row, and label it as
  *their published CSV output*, not a local execution of their tool.

If the table is genuinely too wide for one Markdown table, split **by corpus**
(one table per benchmark, same columns) — never by detector family, which would
separate the cheap detectors out and defeat the point.

## Findings the report must carry

These are established and must survive into the final document. Lead with the
ones that cut against us:

1. **No Axcess detector beats KAFE.** On the 39 scored subjects KAFE posts
   96.0% precision / 100% recall / 25.7 ms per button, F1 98.0%. Best Axcess F1
   is 85.2% (D9+S4ours) at 1353.6 ms/button.
2. **Where Axcess wins is cost** — 29 of 46 rows faster than KAFE; D4 hits 100%
   recall at 0.1 ms/button with 14 FPs against KAFE's 1.
3. **The cheap rules do not transfer.** C15/C16 score 100%/97.4% on fixtures and
   81.8%/85.7% on KAFE. R1, R2, R3 and R9 fire on *nothing* on unseen pages
   (C10=C11=C12, C16=C15). Only R5 and R7/R8 do work, and R7/R8 cost 354.8
   ms/button — over the 300 ms ceiling.
4. **C10–C16 are all zero on gds and ma11y** because each is "C9 minus a rule"
   and C9 abstains there. Those rows measure C9's abstention, not the rules.
5. **The comparison is one-way.** Our detectors ran on their corpus; KAFE never
   ran on ours. Blocked by the un-rebuilt Java stack *and* by a page-level
   vs element-level unit mismatch. This is a deliberate scope decision, not an
   oversight — see `BRIEF-ASYMMETRY.md`.
6. **Denominator**: 60 KAFE subjects → 7 excluded pre-run → 52 attempted → 13
   whole-subject abstentions → 39 scored. Name every exclusion. No abstention
   was ever counted as a negative.
7. Whatever `CAPDIAG-REPORT.md` concluded about the 7 capped subjects — read it
   and carry its verdict.

## The 300 ms ceiling

The CEO's ceiling is **per-button detection latency**, not per-page. Keep the
two units distinct everywhere: measured per-button latency in this harness
versus KAFE's amortised `Detection ÷ visible control nodes` quotient from their
published CSV. Never present the amortised figure as measured per-button
latency, and never imply our per-button numbers and theirs were measured the
same way — theirs is 2019 hardware and a different instrument. Say which
detectors clear 300 ms and which do not.
