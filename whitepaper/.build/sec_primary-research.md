## Validating Demand: The PARI Parent Survey as Primary Research

Every preceding section of this white paper rests on secondary evidence: national assessments, peer-reviewed developmental studies, federal labor projections, and third-party market sizing. That body of evidence is unusually consistent, but it is, by definition, about the market in aggregate, not about PARI STEAM World specifically. The PARI Parent Survey is the company's primary-research layer, designed to convert documented macro-gaps into product-level demand signal. It tests, against real parents, whether the behaviors, anxieties, and willingness-to-pay implied by the dossier actually attach to this concept, this format, and this price.

### From documented gaps to testable hypotheses

The survey is structured so that each section interrogates a specific, pre-registered hypothesis drawn directly from the secondary record. The intent is not to ask parents whether they like the idea, but to triangulate stated demand against the independent benchmarks already established in this paper.

| Section | Hypothesis under test | Dossier anchor it validates against |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Family profile** | The reachable audience skews toward the early-childhood, education-engaged household. | Preschool is the fastest-growing US EdTech sector (Grand View Research, 2024). |
| **B. How children spend time** | Screen time is high and parents are dissatisfied with it. | Kids 0-8 average ~2.5 hours of screen time daily (Common Sense Media, 2025). |
| **C. Parent concerns** | Future-skills anxiety is real and STEM-linked. | 76% of parents want a STEM career for their child (The Toy Association, 2019). |
| **D. STEM attitudes** | Parents rate STEM highly yet see latent, unconverted curiosity in their child. | 75% of Gen Z report STEM interest; only 29% name it a first-choice career (Gallup/Walton, 2023). |
| **E. Purchase behavior** | Parents already spend on educational products, including STEM kits. | US educational toys market ~$12.19B, STEM the fastest sub-segment at 15.0% CAGR (Grand View Research, 2024). |
| **F. Concept test** | The integrated story + kit + AI concept has standalone appeal. | No incumbent integrates all three (Custom Market Insights / competitive analysis, 2024). |
| **G. DIY kit demand** | Specific kit concepts command differentiated interest. | 78% of parents want more creativity/problem-solving toys (The Toy Association, 2025). |
| **H. Role-model validation** | Parents value seeing all genders as scientists. | Female role-model exposure lifts girls' STEM persistence 8-26 pts (J-PAL, 2024). |
| **I. Pricing** | A premium, recurring price is acceptable for a complete experience. | Lovevery lifetime value reached $692.38 per customer (Bloomberg Second Measure, 2021). |
| **J. Open-ended** | Parents will articulate the unmet need in their own words. | Awareness gap: 89% think their child is on track (Gallup/Learning Heroes, 2023). |

### What each cluster is built to prove

**Sections A-C establish the problem in the household.** Section B measures recreational screen time and satisfaction, testing whether the parental ambivalence documented nationally (~75-80% express concern about screen media, Common Sense Media, 2025) shows up among prospective buyers and creates appetite for a screen-light alternative. Section C surfaces the worry that the "B-flation" research shows is latent but easily activated once parents learn the truth (Gallup/Learning Heroes, 2023). These sections exist to confirm that the demand we infer from secondary data is felt, not merely statistical.

**Sections D-E translate concern into category behavior.** Section D separates parental valuation of STEM from the child's observed curiosity, the same interest-to-intent gap that defines the national opportunity (Gallup/Walton, 2023). Section E quantifies actual annual spend and prior STEM-kit purchase, grounding willingness-to-pay in revealed behavior rather than aspiration, and benchmarking it against the ~$12.19B US educational-toys category where STEM is the fastest-growing slice at a 15.0% CAGR (Grand View Research, 2024).

**Sections F-G are the core concept and product test.** Section F scores appeal of the integrated story + kit + AI model on a 1-10 scale and isolates which element drives interest and which raises concern, directly probing the white space no incumbent occupies (competitive analysis, 2024). Section G rates eight discrete kit concepts on a 1-5 scale, producing a demand-ranked roadmap rather than a single yes/no, and operationalizing the documented appetite for hands-on, creativity-building play (78% of parents, The Toy Association, 2025).

**Section H validates the differentiator.** It tests whether parents endorse diverse girl protagonists as role models, the lever with the strongest causal evidence in the dossier: female role-model exposure raises girls' STEM persistence by 8 to 26 percentage points (J-PAL, 2024), and character identification tracks real career behavior (the "Scully Effect": medium/heavy viewers 50% more likely to have worked in STEM, Geena Davis Institute, 2018). This is where social mission and commercial differentiation are jointly stress-tested.

**Section I prices the experience.** It identifies the most-likely package and a reasonable price for a complete STEM story experience, calibrated against proven premium recurring spend in the category (Lovevery's $692.38 lifetime value, Bloomberg Second Measure, 2021) and the broad US enrichment budget already in play.

**Section J is the qualitative goldmine,** capturing unprompted product wishes, disengagement triggers, and parent-designed kits, the open-text layer that turns confirmatory findings into product specification.

### Methodology intent and next-step milestones

The instrument is intentionally mixed-method: closed scales for benchmarkable quantitative comparison against the dossier, paired with open-ended prompts that protect against confirmation bias by letting unmet needs surface unprompted. Each closed item is mapped to an external benchmark, so survey results can be read as convergent or divergent with the national picture rather than in isolation.

The validation roadmap is staged. The near-term milestone is fielding the survey to a sufficient, demographically representative US sample to establish baseline concept appeal (Section F), a demand-ranked kit roadmap (Section G), and an evidenced price band (Section I). Subsequent milestones move from stated to revealed demand: a landing-page or waitlist conversion test, a paid pre-order or pilot cohort to measure real purchase rather than intent, and an early-product engagement and retention study, the metric that most directly answers the churn risk facing single-format subscriptions (more than one-third of US subscribers cancel within three months, McKinsey, 2018). Together these steps move PARI STEAM World from "the secondary data says a gap exists" to "our primary research shows this product fills it."
