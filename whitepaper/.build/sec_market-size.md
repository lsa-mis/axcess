## The Market Opportunity: Sizing the STEAM Learning Economy

PARI STEAM World sits at the convergence of several large, fast-growing US spending pools — educational toys, EdTech, AI-in-education, supplemental tutoring, and hands-on subscription kits — unified by one of the most durable purchase drivers in consumer behavior: a parent's investment in their child's future. This section sizes that opportunity using a disciplined TAM/SAM/SOM framing, drawing only on verified US-specific figures (with North America and global benchmarks clearly labeled where they appear).

### The Layered US Market

The US children's learning economy is not a single market but a stack of segments, each growing well above GDP. At the macro level, the US K-12 education market generated $821.6 billion in 2024 and is projected to reach $1,518.4 billion by 2030 at a 10.8% CAGR (Grand View Research, 2024). Within that, the digital and product layers most relevant to an early-childhood STEAM offering are the following:

Two signals deserve emphasis. First, **early childhood is where the momentum is**: within US EdTech, K-12 is the largest sector but **preschool is the fastest-growing** through 2030 (Grand View Research, 2024) — precisely the under-served age band PARI targets. Second, **AI-driven personalization is the steepest curve in the entire stack**, with US AI-in-education compounding at 31.2% to $8.84 billion by 2030 (Grand View Research, 2024), validating PARI's AI-companion layer as a tailwind rather than a gamble.

### TAM: The Total Spend Pool

We define the total addressable market as US household and institutional spend that an integrated early-STEAM ecosystem can plausibly touch. The most direct envelope is the US educational toys market — $12.19 billion in 2023, growing at 10.8% (Grand View Research, 2024) — sitting alongside adjacent enrichment budgets parents already pay into: the **US total private tutoring market of $39.6 billion in 2024** (Global Industry Analysts, 2024) and the **$47.74 billion US EdTech market** (Grand View Research, 2024). Critically, demand inside the educational-toys category is not evenly distributed: **STEM-toy demand is the fastest-growing sub-segment at a 15.0% CAGR, outpacing the 10.8% category average** (Grand View Research, 2024). STEM is the growth engine, yet remains under-penetrated relative to total educational-toy spend — the white space PARI is built to capture.

### SAM: The STEM-Specific Slice We Serve

The serviceable addressable market narrows to dedicated, hands-on STEM products and the digital learning that complements them. Here the verified numbers are notably smaller than headline figures suggest, which is exactly why the opportunity is real. Pure-play STEM toys are still a niche: the **North America STEM toys market was $425.9 million in 2024**, rising to $629.2 million by 2030 (Grand View Research, 2024), and because the **US holds roughly 80% of that North American market, the US STEM-toys segment is on the order of $340 million in 2024** (Grand View Research, 2024). That a category whose underlying demand grows at 15.0% has only matured to a few hundred million dollars signals room to convert general educational-toy and tutoring buyers into dedicated STEM buyers — rather than a saturated field to fight over.

Layered on top is genuine, durable willingness to pay for **supplemental digital learning**: the US online private tutoring market nearly doubles from $4.33 billion (2024) to $8.09 billion (2030) at 11.1% (Grand View Research, 2025), the same household enrichment budget a subscription STEAM product competes for.

### Parent Willingness-to-Pay: The Demand Engine Beneath the Numbers

Market size means little without confirmed willingness to pay, and the parent-attitudes evidence is unambiguous. **76% of US parents want their child to end up in a STEM-related career** (The Toy Association, 2019), and **94% say toys play a significant role in their child's development while 78% want more toys that build creativity and problem-solving** (The Toy Association, 2023) — demand for developmental, hands-on play over passive entertainment. That preference converts to real, recurring dollars: in the hands-on educational-subscription category, **Lovevery's lifetime sales per customer reached $692.38 after 24 months — more than double KiwiCo's and Little Passports'** (Bloomberg Second Measure, 2021), proof that US parents will pay premium, recurring prices for curated, developmentally-staged STEAM experiences.

The spend is steep but unequal: **high-income US families spend roughly $8,900 per child per year on enrichment versus about $1,300 for the lowest-income quintile — a gap that widened from ~4x to ~7x** (The Hamilton Project, 2018). Even mainstream households budget meaningfully, with back-to-school spending projected at about **$886 per household in 2025** (Empower, 2025). The willingness exists across the income spectrum; affordable, high-quality early STEAM is the lever that broadens access beyond the affluent top quintile.

### SOM: A Realistic Beachhead

The serviceable obtainable market is the share PARI can capture in its early years by owning an unoccupied position — integrated story, kit, and AI companion with diverse girl protagonists — inside a US STEM-toys niche of roughly $340 million (Grand View Research, 2024) that is expanding off a 15.0% demand CAGR (Grand View Research, 2024), while drawing spillover from the multi-billion-dollar US educational-toys ($12.19B), tutoring ($39.6B), and preschool-led EdTech pools. The strategic logic is consistent across every verified layer: **the fastest-growing slices of the US education economy — STEM demand (15.0%), preschool EdTech, and AI-in-education (31.2%) — are precisely the ones least served for early childhood today.** That mismatch between where demand is accelerating and where dedicated products exist is the market opportunity PARI STEAM World is positioned to convert.
